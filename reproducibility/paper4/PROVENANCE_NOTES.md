# Paper IV consolidated-run provenance notes

The published benchmark and Kepler numbers are backed by one consolidated
workspace produced by `scripts/run_paper4_reproduction.py` executing
`protocol.json`. These notes record the code revisions, data provenance,
and the audit trail connecting them, so no claim rests on an undocumented
input.

## Code revisions

- **NestyNet core**: `dc4d7ca3f42c9635774fa67537b2f28bf1df09c4` for every
  step (no core commits during the campaign; the runner now records the
  core's git state per manifest, and steps executed before that field was
  added are covered by the core's quiet commit log across the whole span).
- **NestyNet_SR**: steps record their revision in each workspace manifest.
  The benchmark-relevant code is identical across the span they cover: the
  only commits touching the DE execution path landed before every
  surviving step ran, and interleaved commits were audited file-by-file as
  execution-path inert. The final `scalar_hybrid` arm runs at the
  additive-forcing revision (`b6613d0`), with cases 127, 129 and 130 rerun
  at `ddaaccc` (see below); on the validation trajectories it gives
  55 PASS / 2 PARTIAL / 0 FAIL and `scalar_stlsq` gives 40 / 8 / 9.

## Benchmark data: deterministic regeneration

All scalar, complex, and compositional trajectory data is the seeded,
deterministic output of the generators in this repository; the protocol's
`--skip_generate` reuses files only when present. Three stale local
corpora predating generator fixes were found to poison results
(scalar: pre-support-pilot trajectories containing numerical blow-up;
complex: parameter-unlabeled CSVs mismatching the scorer; compositional:
2048-point files below the fit budget). All three were archived out of the
data directories and are NOT part of the benchmark definition; regenerated
data was verified byte-identical to the paper-era corpora (md5 over all
files, e.g. de121 `046c0ef0…`, complex 216/216 matches). A user starting
from a clean checkout regenerates identical data.

## Kepler surrogate-acceleration cache

`kepler_direct` consumes a content-addressed per-body cache
(`surrogate_accels_1d/`). Cache keys (schema `xyz_v2`, commit `04c673d`)
hash the pre-projection 3D state series plus configuration, making entries
portable across machines: the earlier array-level keys were
platform-sensitive through the orbital-plane eigenbasis (LAPACK last
bits), which cross-platform reuse exposed (184/308 hits). The shipped
cache was fitted on an HPC cluster from source verified sha256-identical
to the revisions above (manifests in the workspace `hpc/` records), with
key portability proven BEFORE compute via
`examples/kepler_ephemeris_real/emit_accel_cache_keys.py` (identical
308-key lists on both machines, empty diff). The consuming run re-validates
every entry by key at load; the final `kepler_direct` execution performed
zero fits. Position-channel certificates are part of the cache
configuration and required by the key.

## Result verification

Every step's summary was diffed case-by-case against
`protocol.json:expected_results` (where recorded) and against the paper's
tables: baseline STLSQ 40/9/8 (57/57 case-identical), hybrid 53/4/0,
complex 23/3/0, compositional 4/4, alias arbitration 4/4, Maxwell
statuses, Noether admission, and the Kepler summary numbers agreeing with
the independent definitive run to the surrogate-nondeterminism floor
(differences in the seventh decimal).

## Known sensitivities

- Surrogate training is not bit-deterministic run-to-run (thread
  scheduling); validation losses on identical seeds can differ while all
  summary-level claims remain stable at their quoted precision.
- Two benchmark cases sit on selection knife-edges where near-tied
  candidates alternate between draws: the complex time-independent
  Schrödinger eigenvalue problem (near-collinear single-mode basis) and,
  before the additive-forcing lane, the driven harmonic oscillator. The
  paper marks the former PARTIAL (the conservative draw). Rollout-scored
  arbitration of near-tied rescue candidates is planned follow-up work.

## Corrected targets (2026-08-20)

Three scalar targets were found to be mis-specified and were corrected in
`data/feynman_de_benchmark.txt` (and, for 129, in the hand-written RHS,
ground truth and units spec of `examples/feynman_de/problem_defs.py`):
the relativistic oscillator (127; the Lorentz factor multiplies the restoring
term), the string exponential branch (129; `mu/T`), and capillary rise (130;
planar meniscus balance with the nonlinear factor on the numerator and
`gamma` rescaled to capillary-length units). Their trajectories were
regenerated by the seeded generator and both scalar arms were rerun for
these three cases only, with the protocol argv, at revision `ddaaccc`; the
superseded per-case records were archived outside the artifact.

## Sealed test

The protocol fits each scalar case on trajectories ic0--ic3 and uses ic4--ic5
both to arbitrate between first-line and rescue candidates (hybrid arm) and
to report the rollout NRMSE, so those two trajectories are a validation set.
After discovery, `scripts/score_paper4_sealed_test.py` extended each case's
seeded initial-condition stream from six to eight draws (ic0--ic5 are
regenerated byte-identically, verified by checksum) and scored the final
reported equation of every case on ic6--ic7 with the benchmark's own
rollout validator and no refitting. The paper reports these sealed marks
(`results_ref/sealed_test/<arm>/summary.json`): STLSQ 39 / 9 / 9 and hybrid
52 / 2 / 3, against 40 / 8 / 9 and 55 / 2 / 0 on the validation
trajectories. The complex arm is scored structurally against the ground-truth
term list; `scripts/score_paper4_sealed_test_complex.py` additionally rolls
out the eleven complex ODE systems on fresh trajectories (11 / 11 PASS).
